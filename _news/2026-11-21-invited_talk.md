On 21 November I was an invited speaker at the PhD Fall School 2026 of the Department of Legal Sciences (Dipartimento di Scienze Giuridiche) at [Sapienza Università di Roma](https://www.uniroma1.it/en/).

I talked about how language models work, the importance of building open-data models such as Minerva, the model developed by my lab, Sapienza NLP, their applications in the legal domain, and, above all, why these tools are fallible and should be used with care, especially in a complex and high-stakes field like law.

It was also the first time I spoke to people from a field entirely different from mine, and the questions and the discussion with the PhD students afterwards showed me that many things we take for granted in AI are not obvious outside our community.
This made me realise the important role that scientific dissemination and open science play: they are two of the pillars of a democratic society, and as such they need to be actively supported and nurtured.

I hope to do more talks like this in the future. Thanks to [Roberta Peleggi](https://corsidilaurea.uniroma1.it/en/lecturer/c9cfc805fa7dcd65de5e1f85866279bfd2e503e14ae49fdc9bdc91b3) for the invitation!
